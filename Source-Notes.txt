THE WORKING DAY: SOURCE NOTES
Law and guidance checked 6 October 2026.

These sources support original fictional teaching examples. No source approves or endorses this course. Your workplace policies decide the approved tools, contacts and actions.

S1: UK National Cyber Security Centre: advice for staff
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/information/top-tips-for-staff
Supports: Staff habits support, but do not replace, security protections on computers and accounts.

S2: UK National Cyber Security Centre: checking deceptive messages
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/phishing
Supports: Use a separate trusted way to check requests, several security protections and supportive reporting.

S3: UK National Cyber Security Centre: protect important accounts
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-organisations-guide-to-cyber-security/secure-your-important-online-accounts
Supports: Use different strong passwords, approved tools that store passwords and extra sign-in checks. Sign-in methods that use your device or a physical key can resist fake login pages.

S4: UK National Cyber Security Centre: extra sign-in checks
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/mfa-for-your-corporate-online-services/why-mfa-matters
Supports: Benefits and limits of a second check when signing in.

S5: UK National Cyber Security Centre: advice for people using work devices
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/end-user-devices-advice-end-users
Supports: Work-managed devices, travel, physical security and reporting loss.

S6: UK National Cyber Security Centre: handling security problems
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/small-business-guidance--response-and-recovery
Supports: Prepare for problems, stop further harm and restore safe work.

S7: UK data protection regulator: personal information problems
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/
Supports: When personal information is lost, wrongly shared, changed, damaged, unavailable or accessed without permission; likely harm, records and who must be told.

S8: UK data protection regulator: first steps after a personal information problem
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/personal-data-breaches/72-hours-how-to-respond-to-a-personal-data-breach/
Supports: Stop further harm, find out what happened and assess the likely harm to people.

S9: UK National Cyber Security Centre: Cyber Essentials approval scheme
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberessentials/overview
Supports: A separate scheme checking security protections on computers and accounts. Staff training is not this approval.

S10: UK data protection regulator: Data (Use and Access) Act 2025
https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/legislation-we-cover/data-use-and-access-act-2025/

S12: UK National Cyber Security Centre: square scanning codes
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/qr-codes-whats-real-risk

S13: US Federal Trade Commission: public wireless networks
https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/are-public-wi-fi-networks-safe-what-you-need-know
Supports: A protected connection does not prove a website or request is trustworthy. This is practical advice, not UK law.

S15: UK National Cyber Security Centre: protected work connections
https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/device-security-guidance/infrastructure/virtual-private-networks

https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4035
Supports: A proposal being considered by Parliament in the checked record, not an existing universal duty for staff.

IMPORTANT LIMITS
Staff should report quickly. A personal-data breach is a security problem involving loss, destruction, change, damage, improper sharing or access to information about identifiable people; unavailability can count too. The organisation deciding why and how it is used assesses regulator reporting, records every personal-data breach and the reasons for its decisions, even when no regulator report is needed, and tells affected people if the risk to them is high. Its required regulator report is due without unnecessary delay and, where possible, within 72 hours of its awareness. An organisation handling that information for it following its instructions tells it without unnecessary delay once aware of a breach, regardless of the regulator reporting risk threshold. It must tell the regulator unless a risk to people's rights and freedoms is unlikely. This assessment includes possible harm to privacy, fraud, discrimination and other harm to the people affected. Neither rule permits staff to wait.
The separate deadline for public phone and internet providers is not a universal staff deadline.
The proposed Cyber Security and Resilience law was still being considered in the checked Parliament record.
A promise to delete information is evidence to assess, not proof that the risk is gone.
Follow workplace instructions for protected connections, device settings, password changes and disconnecting devices.
